# Whale R2 — Slice 6/7: ask-surface — seven signature sparks

> Fresh burn (2026-11-07): 7/7 sparks landed on `deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash` via the
> rebuilt `zen-whale-burn.mjs` rig (16,948 tok this salvo). Same design language and perf law as
> slices 1–2.

Design language (fixed): "A HUD, not a magazine" — ink-blue black #05070e, neon cyan / magenta / electric yellow, self-hosted Electrolize / Inter / JetBrains Mono, sharp corners with one notched edge, corner brackets on focus, circuit grid + scanlines, per-source neon hue via `--dn-src`.

PERF LAW: phones run ZERO animations; desktop at most 3 ambient loops per page; `prefers-reduced-motion` renders every spark's static end-state.

| # | Spark | Intent (feel) | Implementation shape | Loop slot |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Glyph Drain Uplink | Your typed question physically leaves your hands — mass becomes signal, and you feel the corpus receive it. | On submit, the ask-input's value is split into per-character <span data-glyph> nodes (already present from a JS mirror of the field, so no reflow surprise); each glyph gets style.setProperty('--i', idx) and animates transform: translateY(1.0em) with opacity 1→0 over 180ms, delay = min(idx*14, 420)ms, so the sentence visibly *drains* downward into the notched trench beneath the field. A single <canvas class='uplink-rail'> positioned over the trench then runs a one-shot rAF loop: it spawns one 6×6px packet square per whitespace-delimited word (cap 24), each travelling a quadratic bezier from the glyph's drained x-position to the answer-pane anchor, with easeOutCubic over 520–760ms (distance-proportional: t = 1 - (1-p)^3, p = elapsed/dur), leaving a 14px tapering tail drawn as a linear gradient stroke in var(--dn-src). The trench itself flashes a 2px inset rule in --dn-src for 240ms, then decays via a single CSS transition on box-shadow (no loop). Caret becomes a 1px vertical launch rail that snaps to the trench's left edge on submit and returns after the last packet lands; total one-shot budget is capped at 900ms and the canvas is removed on completion. | none (one-shot) |
| 2 | Reasoning Dendrite | You can almost watch the corpus think — orthogonal traces grow out of your question, then fold into the citations they earned. | On submit, an SVG overlay (viewBox matching the ASK panel, pointer-events:none) is injected above the transcript; a seeded PRNG (mulberry32 over a hash of the question string) picks 9–14 branch nodes on an 8px circuit grid so the same question always grows the same tree. Each branch is a single <path> of Manhattan-routed orthogonal segments (H-then-V, corner chamfered 2px) stroked in --dn-src at 1.5px, animated purely by stroke-dasharray/stroke-dashoffset from pathLength to 0 with a per-node delay of 40ms + depth*70ms and a 180ms cubic-bezier(.2,.8,.1,1) ramp, capped at 900ms total. Terminal nodes get a 3px square in neon cyan that pulses exactly twice (a 120ms opacity flip, not an infinite loop) while 'thinking' text sits in JetBrains Mono at 62% opacity. On answer resolve, every path's dashoffset animates back to pathLength over 260ms in reverse child-first order, and each terminal square translates on a fixed bezier to the nearest citation chip anchor (measured once via getBoundingClientRect), handing off to the citation dot — the ghost of the reasoning becomes the receipts. | none (one-shot) |
| 3 | Meridian Dock | The answer doesn't just appear — it earns a spine, and every claim physically tows its source into place. | A 96px-tall canvas overlay (#dnMeridian) sits in the answer column's left gutter; on each SSE token batch Svelte calls drawMeridian(), which rebuilds a single variable-width stroke from sentence y-offsets using ctx.setTransform(dpr,0,0,dpr,0,0) then moveTo/quadraticCurveTo down the gutter — per-sentence width = 1 + 3*clamp((score-0.35)/0.65,0,1), drawn twice: a 0.18-alpha hairline in var(--dn-src) and an opaque overlay whose lineWidth lerps toward the target at 0.18/frame (frame loop lives ONLY while a stream is open, cancelled on 'done'). Inline citation chips render early as dimmed spans with transform:scale(.92) and are measured with getBoundingClientRect; at stream end, each chip FLIPs out of the prose and docks onto the meridian at its sentence's y-offset in document order, staggered 60ms apart over 480ms cubic-bezier(0.16,1,0.3,1), the vacated inline slot collapsing with letter-spacing:-0.5px on the neighbours. After all chips settle, one 700ms pass draws 1px bezier leader curves (ctx.bezierCurveTo with control points at 40% of the horizontal gap) from the sentence's last word baseline into each dock node, each curve's stroke-dashoffset animating 0→full length once, and the meridian dims to 0.45 alpha behind the docked column. Hovering a dock node calls redrawSegment(i), repainting that sentence's meridian slice at full saturation plus a single 120ms 2px flash tick at the dock's baseline — event-driven, no ambient loop, canvas re-allocated on resize via one ResizeObserver. | none (event-driven) |
| 4 | Fuse-Seam Answer Trace | The answer arrives like a live weld that cools into a ruled margin — latency is readable as heat. | A single canvas.dn-ask__seam overlays the answer gutter, drawing a 2px spline from the question's bottom bracket to the current token caret. Every token event pushes {x,y,heat:1} and sets lastToken=performance.now(); each rAF tick (throttled to 30fps while streaming) maps heat = clamp(1 - (now-lastToken)/800, 0, 1), colors the segment lerp(#e8ff3a, #22e6ff, 1-heat), and sets width 1+2*heat. Citation spans with [data-src] spawn a perpendicular 10px stub node colored var(--dn-src); stubs jitter ±2px until stream end. On done, one 240ms cubic-bezier(.2,.8,.2,1) pass lerps every point to x=gutterX, heat→0, and snaps stubs to 90°, leaving a clean 1px cyan rule with tick marks. The answer text uses mask-image linear-gradient(180deg,#000 var(--seam-y),transparent calc(var(--seam-y) + 48px)) so text surfaces behind the seam. | none (event-driven) |
| 5 | Citation Suture Loom | The answer sews itself to its evidence—each claim pulls a visible thread to the source that proves it. | On each new `.dn-ask__answer` token with `[data-cite-id]`, find the paired `.dn-ask__source[data-cite-id]` and append the pair to a single `.dn-ask__suture-layer > canvas` overlay sized to the panel. Use `getBoundingClientRect()` anchors from token underline to source-card notch, then draw a 1px quadratic Bézier with control point offset by `(dy * 0.35, dx * 0.12)` plus a hash-derived jitter of ±6px so every suture is slightly idiosyncratic. Animate each new path one-shot over 180ms by tweening `lineDashOffset` from pathLength to 0, stroke `--dn-src` at 55% alpha, and terminate with a 3px dot plus a 6px corner-bracket tick at the source notch. Hovering `.dn-cite-chip` sets `data-active`, re-strokes only that suture in 90ms at 90% alpha, and lights the matching source card’s corner brackets; no rAF runs after settle, and resize recalculates paths once. | none (event-driven) |
| 6 | Ink-Press Commit | Each word of the answer slams in too heavy and then relaxes — the paragraph physically exhales as it fills. | Every streamed token is wrapped by a Svelte action into <span class="dn-w">; on insertion it gains .is-land, which runs a 320ms one-shot @keyframes dn-inkpress: font-variation-settings wght 400→680 at 30%→400, letter-spacing 0→0.1em→0, color #eaf6ff→var(--dn-src), and a 0→2px→0 text-shadow halo in the source hue — the word presses in, then relaxes. At the same frame a ::before 7px tick is appended to the left commit rail .dn-rail and slides from translateX(-7px) to 0 with a hard 1px leading edge, stacking at 6px intervals so the rail grows like a receipt printing down the answer's spine. When the last token lands, .dn-answer.is-complete fires one 480ms pass: an ::after 2px scan bar (linear-gradient cyan→transparent, mix-blend-mode:screen) translates Y 0→100% once, and the four corner brackets flip opacity 0→1 with steps(1,end) — a snap, not a fade. If the stream exceeds ~40 tok/s the action coalesces tokens into batches of 3 with a 12ms stagger, capping style recalcs and keeping the rail's tick cadence readable. | none (event-driven) |
| 7 | Proof-Press Inking | The answer feels stamped into the panel by a machine, not typed by a chatbot. | On answer commit, Svelte 5 sets data-ink=pressing on .ask-answer and inserts one .proof-platen div: 1px cyan top edge, 7px notched right edge, absolute full-width. CSS keyframes platen-press translateY(0) to translateY(100%) over 520ms cubic-bezier(.2,.8,.2,1), while each .answer-line gets --i from its index and animates color from rgba(234,246,255,.08) to #eaf6ff plus -webkit-text-stroke 1px rgba(0,255,255,.35) to 0 with delay calc(80ms + var(--i) * 22ms). After the last line, .cite-chip transitions scale .92 to 1 and opacity .4 to 1 with 70ms stagger; the platen fades out in 120ms, then onanimationend removes data-ink. Total run 620ms; only transform, opacity, color, and text-stroke animate, with no canvas and no ambient loop. | none (one-shot) |

## Perf & motion budget

**1. Glyph Drain Uplink** — loop slot: none (one-shot). Phones: Glyphs are simply absent from the field and the trench shows a static dotted trace with word-count ticks parked at the answer anchor. Reduced motion: Query text appears instantly in the trench as a single static packet row and the rail renders as a completed dotted path with one solid --dn-src tick.

**2. Reasoning Dendrite** — loop slot: none (one-shot). Phones: The dendrite renders fully drawn at rest for 200ms, then dissolves to the citation dots with no motion. Reduced motion: Skip the grow and retract entirely: the completed tree appears at full opacity for 200ms and is replaced by the citation dots in a single instant swap.

**3. Meridian Dock** — loop slot: none (event-driven). Phones: Draws the completed meridian, docked chips and leader curves in a single paint on mount — no width animation, no FLIP, no rAF. Reduced motion: Same static end-state at first paint: full-length meridian at final widths, chips pre-docked in the gutter, leader curves already stroked, hover highlight swaps colour instantly with no flash tick.

**4. Fuse-Seam Answer Trace** — loop slot: none (event-driven). Phones: Static 1px cyan rule with citation tick stubs, no canvas loop, text fully revealed. Reduced motion: Renders the settled rule and tick stubs immediately; no heat color, no spline travel, no jitter.

**5. Citation Suture Loom** — loop slot: none (event-driven). Phones: Renders static 1px sutures and terminal dots once after the answer completes; no dash animation, and active citation state becomes a persistent outline. Reduced motion: All sutures render instantly at final dash offset with no re-stroke, jitter animation, or hover animation.

**6. Ink-Press Commit** — loop slot: none (event-driven). Phones: Words paint instantly at wght 400 with no halo, the commit rail arrives pre-drawn at full tick height, and corner brackets ship already lit. Reduced motion: All tokens render at final typography on first paint, the rail is a solid 1px full-height rule, and the arrival scan bar is never created in the DOM.

**7. Proof-Press Inking** — loop slot: none (one-shot). Phones: The answer is fully inked with a static 1px cyan left rule and citation chips in final alignment; no platen or stagger plays. Reduced motion: All answer lines render fully filled with citation chips in final positions; the platen is suppressed entirely.

## Why it surprises

**Glyph Drain Uplink** — The input never fades — it *falls*, which reads as the question being handed down a chute rather than typed at a screen. Packet count equals word count, so a rambling question visibly costs more bandwidth across the rail, and users notice their phrasing getting 'expensive'. The caret briefly becoming a launch rail is the tell that the field was never a text box, it was a breech.

**Reasoning Dendrite** — Nobody expects the thinking indicator to be deterministic — ask the same question twice and the identical branching lattice whispers back, so the machine feels like it has a shape rather than a spinner. Then the traces don't just vanish; they physically walk to the citation chips and become them, collapsing the usual gap between 'it reasoned' and 'here is proof' into one continuous gesture. The whole thing is one SVG, one seeded PRNG, and dasharray math — no canvas, no layout thrash, legal at zero ambient loops.

**Meridian Dock** — Most Q&A UIs treat citations as footnotes you scroll away from; here the citations climb out of the prose and become a spatial index welded to the left edge, so the answer's evidence has a physical address you can point at. The confidence meridian means you can read the answer's trustworthiness as a silhouette before you read a single word of it — thickness is a claim's weight, not decoration. And the collapse of the vacated inline slot re-flows the sentence around the missing chip, so the paragraph literally tightens once its sources have been filed.

**Fuse-Seam Answer Trace** — The seam's color is a latency thermometer — a slow model visibly cools from yellow to cyan between tokens, so you feel stalls before the spinner explains them. Citations don't pop in; they tack sideways like weld stubs and only square up when the answer finishes, making the settle feel mechanical. It turns a text stream into a cooling metal seam that leaves a permanent ruled margin, not a decorative progress bar.

**Citation Suture Loom** — It treats citations as physical stitches, not footnotes—the answer literally cannot detach from its evidence without a visible tear. The hash-jittered control points make the thread lattice look grown and claim-specific rather than templated. Hovering one citation tightens only its thread while the rest hold still, turning provenance into a glanceable inspection surface.

**Ink-Press Commit** — The answer arrives bolder than it is permitted to stay — each word lands at wght 680 with extra tracking and deflates to 400, so a long answer reads as a paragraph exhaling in public rather than a stream of inert characters. The commit rail is a receipt with exactly one tick per accepted token, the only place in the interface that exposes acceptance granularity at word scale. Most streaming text is binary, absent or present; here arrival has a pressure curve and a physical ledger.

**Proof-Press Inking** — Ask panels almost always stream tokens left-to-right, so a single mechanical press pass makes the answer read as printed evidence rather than chat output. The late-arriving citation chips act like registration pins, briefly making provenance feel physical instead of metadata-like.
